Active \= A, Terminated \= T, Pending \= P  
A and T are industry standard value for Account Status. Any other statuses are our internal.  
   
Client Name, Nominee and Intermediary: those are only 3 account designation types in Canada.  
Each account designation type has rules.  
Generally, Client Name: means the Account at the fund company is held directly under the client (the investor�s) name  
Nominee: the account at the fund company is held under the dealer name and the client is the beneficiary owner  
Intermediary: the account at the fund company is held under the intermediary company name and the client is the beneficiary owner  
   
If client name account: the fund company is responsible to send at year end any contribution receipt or tax related to the government for the clients and to the clients  
If Nominee, then the dealers have to do it.  
If Intermediary then the intermediary firm has to do it.  
and there are many other rules.  
   
For example if a client sell a fund from a RRSP plan (Registered Retirement Saving Plan):  
If client name account: the $ is sent to the client, and the amount will be added to T4RSP to be sent to CRA at the year end report (Canada Revenue Agency \= So thue Canada)  
If Nominee: the $ is sent to the dealer, and it depends if the client want to get the $ or reinvest in something else. If invest in something else and also RRSP then no tax consequences if teh client get the $ then there is tax consequence.  
The same with Intermediary  
....

The term Account and Plan are exchange-able depending on the context.  
At the fund company, everything is account based  
At the dealer level: plan is a group of accounts with similar characteristics(the same type, the same investment objective, the same beneficiaries ect)

As for Joint plan:  
An OPEN plan type can have joint owners (usually husband and wife), it is just like a bank account.  
In our system, there is a Primary and secondary owner, it is nothing to do with the rule, it is to do with the user dealing with the clients.  
For the rule, both owners are equal (could be many owners, not just 2\)  
   
The KYC info on the plan level is for the plan, and the KYC info on the client level is for the client.  
Investment objective and Risk Tolerance are on the plan level.  
Client might have one plan with high risk while having another one with low risk...  
   
Joint Plan and Spousal Plan have nothing to do with each other.  
   
Joint Plan is a joint ownership of the plan, and it is applicable only to OPEN plan type  
   
Spousal Plan: is when the Spouse contributes to the plan, and it is applicable only to registered plan like RRSP (Registered Retirement Saving Plan)  
For example the husband contributes $5000 to the spousal plan where his wife is an owner. The husband now can deducts $5000 from his income for personal tax for the year.  
   
Joint reps: when 2 or more reps share commissions.  
Rep 1: 30%  
Rep 2: 50%  
   
Then when the account earns $100 then Rep1 gets $30 and Rep2 gets $50
